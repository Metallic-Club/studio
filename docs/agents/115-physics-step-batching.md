# Physics pipeline batching: issue 115

Status: implemented; focused physics tests passed; full workspace gates pending.

## Goal

Reduce full Rapier pipeline work in ordinary frames while retaining the 1/240 s elapsed-time quantum, character
behavior, and finer contact sampling where it is needed. This branch starts from main independently of issue 116.

## Source verification

Checked the actual dependency pinned by Cargo.lock, Rapier 0.36.1:

- `src/dynamics/integration_parameters.rs`: default `num_solver_iterations` is four; CCD substeps are a separate
  parameter. The public [Rapier integration guide](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/integration_parameters/)
  also calls the solver iterations substeps.
- `src/dynamics/solver/staged_island_solver/init.rs`, lines 77-119: each solve group derives its integration substep
  duration by dividing the pipeline timestep by the solver iteration count plus per-group extra iterations.
- Solver integration substeps do not rerun the full collision pipeline. The reduction cannot be treated as a
  mechanically equivalent replacement for four collision steps.

No third-party engine or Rapier implementation code was copied.

## Contact experiments and resulting policy

Added six behavior probes against the unchanged baseline first; all passed. A naive batching implementation then
failed three of them: a thin sensor crossed by a kinematic mover, a thin sensor during a catch-up frame, and the
Touched event for a fast dynamic box meeting a thin wall. The wall still stopped the box, but its contact event was
lost in that frame. Character tests alone would not have established contact compatibility.

The resulting implementation:

- Keeps the existing 1/240 s accumulator. A 144 Hz call consumes available time immediately rather than waiting for
  a 60 Hz tick; tiny deltas accumulate, invalid deltas contribute zero, and MAX_STEP still clamps long frames.
- Batches at most four quanta into one pipeline call, at most 1/60 s each, with four solver integration substeps.
- Keeps legacy 1/240 s collision sampling if a non-character sensor exists or a non-character anchored mover moves
  or rotates. Character contacts are generated by the character controller and are excluded from the physics queue.
- Also keeps the finer cadence when an awake dynamic body's estimated linear/angular motion plus gravity across
  the catch-up interval could cross its smallest actual collider half extent within one batched tick. Bounds come
  from the actual collider, so clamped sizes and non-box shapes use their physical dimensions.
- Exposes `MAX_PIPELINE_STEP` and `last_step_pipeline_steps()` for documented timing policy and operation counts.

The fallback is intentionally conservative. A scene with gameplay sensors or moving platforms can retain all its
former outer steps. This is not a CCD guarantee or a claim of bit-identical integration across different frame
cadences. Optimizing those cases further needs swept/contact sampling designed separately.

## Evidence so far

- `cargo test -p metallic_physics -j 4`: 11 unit tests, all 18 character tests, and all 16 existing physics tests passed.
- Ordinary 60 Hz frames: one pipeline call instead of four. Empty 30 Hz and 0.25 s catch-up frames use two and 15
  calls respectively. Risky sensor/mover frames retain four and 60 respectively. These are deterministic operation
  counts, not wall-clock benchmarks or a 3x throughput claim.
- High-refresh and tiny-delta probes verify immediate consumption, residual bounds, and expected gravitational
  velocity; fast-contact probes verify the fallback preserves the events that naive batching lost.
